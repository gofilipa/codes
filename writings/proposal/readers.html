1.2. key issues

Below are my responses to the three key issues outlined in your email.

1.2.1. How does the argument accumulate over the chapters?

Language technologies reduce language complexity to make it computable. Words are removed from their original context, cleaned and standardized, so that they can be stored and analyzed at scale. These increasingly automated processes encode dominant logics and extractive incentives onto language data, creating well-documented issues with data sovereignty, privacy, and most recently, bias, as current AI tools demonstrate.

This book breaks open and re-works technologies for analyzing language about sex, gender and sexuality. It takes a close look into technologies like web scraping, text analysis, machine learning, among others. Then, drawing from concepts in Queer Studies and related fields, it re-works these technologies into new analytical methodologies that open up, rather than reduce, novel readings of language data.

Each of the five chapters considers a different technology for working with language. For that technology, the chapter identifies a technical constraint that reduces or flattens language meaning. For example, chapter 2, "Text Analysis," explores the technology of quantitative text analysis, and within that technology, the constraint of iteration: a logic of repetition in which text analysis programs execute the same action across large collections of text, one word at a time. This constraint, which allows quantitative text analysis to clean and regularize texts en masse, supresses minor details in language forms in favor of the most frequent textual patterns. The chapter then takes Judith Butler's concept of Gender Performativity, which describes gender as a social and behavioral phenomenon that becomes intelligible through habit or repetition, to re-work computational iteration into a new text analysis practice for studying subtle gender markers and descriptions in language. Unlike typical text analysis projects, which prioritize dominant semantic patterns and relationships, this new methodology uses quantification to surface the idiosyncracies and nuances in language meaning.

The rest of the chapters similarly re-work computational processes and their constraints by applying a conceptual tool from Queer Studies. The table below sketches these dynamics.

Each chapter tests its methodology on a different literary object. The earlier chapters explore traditional works of queer literature (Gloria Anzaldúa's Borderlands/La Frontera: The New Mestiza, 1987; Virginia Woolf’s Orlando: A Biography, 1928; Oscar Wilde’s The Picture of Dorian Gray, 1890), and the latter chapters take on more eclectic source material, some of which features heterosexual characters, from science fiction, electronic literature and reality TV (Octavia Butler's Dawn, 1987; Entropy8Zuper!'s skinonskinonskin, 1999; Love is Blind, 2020+). While the early chapters showcase novel readings of canonical works, the expansion of source material in the latter chapters poseS new orientations around queerness based on embodiment and shared experience. Echoing Butler's famous call for queerness which is "never fully owned, but always and only redeployed, twisted, queered from a prior usage and in the direction of urgent and expanding political purposes" this book illustrates how digital tools and methods, rather than offering solutions for understanding queerness or making it legible, creates opportunities for exploring its ever shifting permutations. (173).

Re-defining "queer" for the future involves not just expanding subject matter, but also developing a robust conceptual toolset for doing resistance work. As the earlier chapters show, Queer Studies' initial (and necessary) emphasis on identity politics offers resistance methods that operate from within systems of power. The first three chapters thus explore ways for re-working protocols and structures in order to deconstruct and reassemble totalizing logics. However, the latter chapters, which draw from more recent Trans- and Black Feminist-inflected work in Queer Studies, foreground methodologies that work alongside and beyond these systems. These methodologies center material realities and embodied experiences in ways that are not (and that cannot be) contained within existing systems. The book ultimately argues that both approaches, working inside and working beyond—shifting between opposition and collaboration, from co-opting to co-creating—are necessary for resistance.

This book's focus on transforming theory into conceptual tools for doing DH analysis (explained in more detail in the Methodology question) sets it apart from other works in the Queer DH field, which explicitly emphasize queer subjects and/or objects of analysis.

1.2.2. What is the urgency?

Language technologies are increasingly black-boxed and automated. This book offers a blueprint for how language technologies work from the inside. It examines a "stack" of language technologies that progress from preliminary to advanced, from gathering language data, to cleaning and processing it, to visualizing and displaying it. The book begins with three chapters on the preliminary processes of gathering and structuring text, as well as exploratory analysis ("Text Capture," "Text Analysis," "Text Preservation"). These chapters introduce founational concepts of text quantification and textual markup (i.e., of counting word frequencies and structuring text data), which the later chapters develop into more advanced and layered processes of screen display and generative AI ("Text Display," "Text Generation").

This scaffolded approach progressively builds a knowledge base over the course of the book. Through exposure to the logics of computational procedures, readers from various disciplinary background learn technical strategies to re-work the all but inevitable language technologies that proliferate around them.

1.2.3. What is the methodological intervention?

In response to the reviewer's comment about the close reading methodology working against the book's larger goal of introducing to digital tools: I actually see the two of them working in tandem.

Close reading is the means for introducing digital tools, making them legible in a Literary Studies register. The book applies close reading to examine the inner workings of digital processes, to excavate how these processes work on the level of technological mechanism. It then seeks analogues for their mechanisms from the field of Queer Studies, which is rich with strategies for destabilizing, resisting and co-opting authoritative and delimiting structures. It applies these strategies from Queer Studies as conceptual tools for technical experimentation—using them to re-work constraining mechanisms into new methodologies that open up novel readings of language data.

I am inspired by Matthew Kirschenbaum's approach in his book, Mechanisms: New Media And The Forensic Imagination. In that book, Kirschenbaum offers deep readings of computer hardware, like the hard drive and the floppy disk, to the level of nanoscale. I studied with Kirschenbaum during my MA at U of Maryland, and he advised me on the first digital project that I ever created. I see my own methodology as expanding his literary approach for close reading digital phenomena into the realm of Queer Studies.
