** reverse outline v1
Introduction:
- text analysis evacuates subtle markers of gender from VW's Orlando
  - (add: gender construct here draws from imperialist violence)
- *thesis: this chapter troubles text analysis's standardization of
  language (including the gender binary) by re-working iteration*

Falsifiable:
- current text analysis methods focus on ambitious questions about
  literary history;
  - these methods reduce the object of study to abstract
    forms/relations.
  - in the case of gender, they perpetuate existing paradigms the
    gender binary.
- by contrast, some methods use text analysis to reflect the mutually
  constructed nature of computation and gender.

Iterative:
- In gender performativity, "iteration" is a way of moving/stepping
  aside from totalizing and repetitive logics like compulsory gender.
- In computer programming, "iteration" allows you to apply one action
  en masse, to a large group.
- How can we take this iterativity and use it to repeat differently?

Fluid:
- Subversion is about *repeating with a difference*. So we repeat our
  similarity computations with a slight difference, by shifting
  between close and distant reading.
- Close reading of Woolf's text brings out:
  - a crisis of signification converges with gender
    ambivalence/ambiguity.
  - gender and language are closely correlated to imagination. 
- This gets at gender as a proliferative system of signification; it
  does not get at the body of gender.
  - critique from Tinseley's notion of fluidity.
