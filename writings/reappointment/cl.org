Dear Members of the SI Peer Review Committee,

Kindly accept this letter and submitted materials as my application
for third-year reappointment as Assistant Professor in the School of
Information at Pratt Institute. Enclosed you will find my curriculum
vitae, as well as teaching, research, and service statements, and
copies of my course syllabi, evaluations, selected student projects,
curriculum development documents, and my most recent research
articles.

Since joining Pratt, I have developed a research agenda that primarily
focuses on using digital methods to study gender, sex, and sexuality
in language. In my teaching, I have developed courses that bring
together humanistic approaches with programming, machine learning, and
digital humanities methods. I have also contributed to Pratt through
service to faculty committees, the Pratt Faculty Union, and to student
groups.

I will briefly highlight several accomplishments from my first two
years at Pratt.

With regard to teaching: since Fall 2024, I have taught five courses
across the School of Information: INFO 601: Foundations of
Information; INFO 664: Programming Cultural Heritage; INFO 657:
Digital Humanities; INFO 697: Special Topics—How to Build a Bot; and
INFO 656: Machine Learning. Across these courses, I have worked to
develop a teaching practice that brings together computational and
humanistic skills, with particular attention to close reading,
writing, and the contingent nature of knowledge.

With regard to research: since joining Pratt, I have established a
sustained interdisciplinary agenda connecting queer and trans studies,
digital humanities, and AI ethics. As a language scholar trained
primarily in English literature, I use computational tools to examine
how language related to gender, sex, and sexuality is represented,
processed, and transformed through information technologies. A central
concern of this work is understanding how computational mechanisms
shape what can be seen, represented, and said in language data.

From Fall 2024 through Fall 2026, I have written five sole-author
papers, two of which have been published in peer-reviewed venues and
three of which are currently under review. Additionally, my book
project, /Queer Code: Re-working Language Technologies/, is expected
to be published in late 2027 or early 2028. Across these projects, I
have sought to communicate across fields that do not always share
methods or assumptions.

In addition to my research and teaching, I have participated in
faculty committees, taskforces, and the Pratt Faculty Union, as well
as contributed to external broader scholarly communities as a
reviewer.

Taken together, my work has allowed me to build an exciting momentum,
one which I am eager to further as a continued member of the Pratt SI
faculty.

Thank you for your consideration.

Sincerely,
